# Swoop: market research (01)

Date: 26 Oct 2026. Scope: is the fold-to-dive / glide lane open on iPhone Duo, name check, Tiny Wings lessons, book-pose and fold-blur evidence. Items marked [unverified] were not seen first-hand.

## 1. Is anyone doing fold-to-dive / glide / Tiny Wings momentum?

**Short answer: no direct competitor found.** Nobody on Duo (or Galaxy/Pixel foldables) is doing a bird that dives on hills using the hinge. The closest are a swing-pumping marble game and two Flappy Bird clones.

| App | What it actually is | Overlap with Swoop |
|---|---|---|
| **Valley Run** (Adam Bellow, $0.99, App Store id6821073675) | A neon **marble swing** game, not hill-sliding. "Fold as the marble turns and it climbs higher, just like pumping a swing." 100 courses in 10 worlds, Perfect timing multipliers, "Lip Stall", music course builder, Game Center. Other iPhones use tilt. World 10 has hills that slow you going up and fling you going down. 3 ratings (5.0). FoldHunt flags that Duo support is unverified: the screenshots show ordinary iPhones. | **Medium.** It also turns fold timing into momentum, and **its website title is "Valley Run: Fold to Fly"**. Different fantasy (marble on a course, no bird, no open hills, no glide). |
| **DuoBird** (Artem Novichkov, open source on GitHub) | A Flappy Bird clone. Opening the hinge 10° past a threshold counts as one flap, and it must close 5° before the next. Not confirmed on the App Store. | Low. Discrete flap, no momentum, no dive. |
| **Foldy Bird** (lyra.horse, web, Jan 2026) | A Flappy Bird clone for Android foldables in Chrome. Each unfold is a tap. Press coverage framed it as "break your foldable faster". | Low. Same flap mechanic. Shows that hinge wear is a press angle. |
| Uphill (Bala Technologies) | A roll-ball bowling game. The hinge angle sets the lane slope, and the slope locks while the ball rolls. | Low. |
| FoldFling | Bend the phone to charge a paper catapult, open it to launch. | Low. A launch, not sustained flight. |
| Dear Pilot | An airline-management sim. Duo support is developer-claimed, with no hinge control shown. | None. |
| Marbles Unhinged / Fold: Gravity Puzzle | Folding tilts the floor or ramp. | Low. |

**Keyword sweep** (glide, soar, wing, bird, dive, swoop, fly, kite, paper plane, hill, slope, surf):
- The FoldHunt list (~110 Duo titles) has no glide, kite, surf or bird-flight game. Its only flying theme is Dear Pilot.
- The DuoDiscover listing mentions no birds, gliding or hills. Its only flying game is Evacopter, a pixel helicopter game with no hinge control.
- Web searches found nothing hinge-controlled for kites, paper planes or gliders on any foldable.
- Samsung Flex Mode game guidance is all about split UI (game on top, controls below), not the hinge as input.

**Takeaway:** the lane is clear, but the two pitches sit close together. Valley Run = "fold timing pumps momentum" (a marble). Swoop = "fold to tuck, open to lift" (a bird over endless hills). Differentiate on the fantasy (wings = the two halves), on continuous glide rather than discrete pumps, and on the sunset/daily-run structure. **Do not use "Fold to Fly".**

**Recommendation: "Swoop: Tuck & Soar"** or **"Swoop: Fold to Soar"**. Both keep the friendly, verb-led brand and tell people what the hinge does, without Valley Run's "Fold to Fly". "Tuck & Soar" also teaches the two inputs (tuck = dive, open = soar). **Fallback: "Foldwing"** if we want a unique one-word name. Check App Store Connect name availability at reservation time, because it is the only authoritative test.

## 3. Tiny Wings and momentum games: design and IP notes

**Tiny Wings (Andreas Illiger, solo developer)**
- Launched 18 Feb 2011 at **$0.99**. It hit **#1 overall paid in the US about a week later**, displacing Angry Birds, with no marketing, purely through word of mouth.
- Game Center stats show 10.5M+ iPhone and 1.8M+ iPad sales [figure undated].
- Prices since: €0.79 at launch, later about €1.99–2.29 [regional, undated]. HD version July 2012, tvOS 2016, **Tiny Wings+ on Apple Arcade 15 Oct 2021** (no ads or IAP).
- Metacritic 85 (HD 92), TouchArcade 5/5, D.I.C.E. Casual Game of the Year nominee. Scott Forstall showed it at WWDC 2011.

**Why it satisfies** (from reviews and design notes):
- One input with deep timing. The skill is releasing at the bottom of a valley so the next upslope launches you.
- A bird that "can't fly yet" makes every launch feel earned.
- A day-cycle timer (sleep at nightfall) creates natural run length and urgency [mechanic from my own knowledge, not in the sources fetched; unverified].
- Procedural landscapes that change daily, so there's always a reason to come back. This is a precedent for our daily seed.
- A deliberately positive, non-destructive tone. Illiger's test was whether a 1.5-year-old could play it.
- The core mechanic came from Nathan McCoy's Wavespark, so the hill-momentum mechanic itself is a shared genre convention. Dune!, Ski on Neon, Dragon Fly!, Tiny Bee and Alto's Adventure/Odyssey all use it. Alto (tap to jump, hold to flip, Zen mode) is the main "momentum + mood" hit of the late 2010s and 2020s.

**Clone and IP risk**
- Mechanics aren't protectable, but expression is. PocketGamer called an Android "Tiny Wings" that copied the bird, island art, title screen, icon and name "blatant infringement". "Tiny Wings Fly" (Fan Bang) used the same bird and a similar logo.
- Avoid:
  - a round, plump little bird with tiny stubby wings on soft pastel hills
  - the "Tiny Wings" name or any "Tiny ___ Wings" wordplay
  - "islands" as level units
  - "fever" or nest-upgrade terminology
  - the "sleep at sunset" framing word-for-word
- Our "distance before sunset" is close to Tiny Wings' nightfall. Consider a different clock, such as a tide, a migrating flock to keep up with, or wind dying down, or at least a visibly different presentation.
- I found no evidence of trademark enforcement [unverified].

## 4. Book pose and fold blur

**Book pose for games**
- No review I found tests gaming comfort in book pose. Apple's partly-folded demo reel does include gaming clips.
- Daring Fireball calls the Duo "a two-handed device even when being used folded". It weighs 254 g, and open it "feels featherweight light", about 15% lighter than an iPad mini. That's encouraging for a two-hand, open hold.
- Other reviewers had mixed views: some wrist fatigue in one-handed use, while one found it more comfortable than an 18 Pro Max.
- Apple's guidance for the partly-folded book pose: content moves off the curve, and controls move to the sides. Good news for us, because our thumbs sit on the outer edges and the sky and horizon can span the crease.
- Hinge-heavy games draw "wear your hinge" coverage (Foldy Bird). One reviewer machine-folded a Duo 32,000+ times.
- Swoop uses small, continuous deflections (roughly 180° down to 140°), not full open/close cycles. This is probably much gentler on the hinge, and a good line for marketing [unverified].

**Blur when folding from flat**
- Press describe a deliberate system blur-and-stretch transition when **opening or closing**, between the outer and inner displays (Gulf News, PhoneArena, the Empirical Health reconstruction).
- Daring Fireball saw a "purposeful blur across the crease" in some apps in V- or L-shaped poses.
- **Our own device test (Brim, 26 Oct):** starting to fold from fully flat blurs one fixed physical half for about 2 s. It shows in screen recordings and also in Photos, it's system-level, and we found no opt-out API.
- Apple's forum guidance: hinge update rate and precision are "system policy", so don't rely on a particular frequency. Accuracy is about 5°.
- **Implication for Swoop:** if "wings fully open" = flat (180°), every dive from flat may trigger a 2 s half-screen blur. That would be fatal for a twitchy momentum game.
- **Mitigation to test first:** make the neutral or "soar" pose about 160–165° (slightly folded, never flat), map dive to about 130–150°, and treat any reading of 175° or more as "max lift" without needing it. Validate on hardware before any art work.

## Top risks
1. **Fold blur from flat.** The core input may fire the system blur. Mitigate by never resting at 180°, and prototype on a real Duo first.
2. **Positioning next to Valley Run** ("Fold to Fly", fold-timed momentum). Differentiate by name, fantasy and feel.
3. **Tiny Wings look-alike risk** (round bird, pastel hills, the sunset clock). Choose a distinct art direction and run-clock framing.
4. Hinge input runs at system-set rates with about 5° accuracy. Smooth and low-pass the angle, and design for coarse input.
