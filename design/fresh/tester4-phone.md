## My four answers
1. **Finger:** press and hold while the bird goes down a wave (wings tucked, it speeds up), and keep holding to the
   bottom. Let go to fly up off the next wave. In the air, hold to come down sooner, let go to glide, and try to touch
   down on the down-sloping side of a wave. I think that's the whole game.
2. **Storm:** the dark rain cloud chasing me from behind (left). The bar at the top shows how close it is. Perfect
   landings push it back. If it catches me, the flight ends.
3. **Going:** along a coast of lighthouses, out to sea and away from the storm: Gull Point 4 km, Saltlight 9,
   Kestrel Head 15, Old Harry 22... and it never ends.
4. **Success:** distance, lighthouses reached, Perfects (and "chains" of them?). **Failure:** the storm catching
   you. I never saw that moment happen, only "Caught" on the results.

## Would I play again tomorrow?
Probably, yes, a couple of times. It's gorgeous and calm, like Monument Valley with a bit of Two Dots timing. Being
2.17 km short of Kestrel Head is a good nag, and a Daily Flight suits me (Wordle habit). It's one-finger and simple once
it clicks. What could stop me: if the storm feels unfair, or the menus keep throwing badges at me.

## Did I buy a lesser version?
A little, yes, but only because of one line: "Fun fact: on iPhone Duo, you fold the phone to fly" (s16). Until then I
felt it was made for my phone. That line tells me the "real" way to play is on a phone I don't own. I paid £1.99 too,
so I'd rather not be reminded.
