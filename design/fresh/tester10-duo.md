# Swoop: Tuck & Soar - first session (tester 10, iPhone Duo)

44, creative director. Love Monument Valley, Alto's Odyssey, Sky, Apple's design. Paid £1.99. No info beyond the screens.

## What I understood

**1. My hands.** I hold the Duo open like a book, and the two halves are the gull's wings (s01, s02). When the bird is
going down the front of a wave, I fold the phone partly shut. That tucks the wings and makes it dive (s03-s05).
I keep it folded until the bottom (s06), then open it flat to spread the wings and fly up off the slope (s07). I land on
the next wave's back slope at the ring (s08). It's one fold per wave, in a rhythm (s10). At the start, I hold it open and
steady during the countdown (s14).

**2. The storm.** A rain cloud follows from behind, on the right. The bar at the top shows how close it is: bird icon,
yellow-to-red gradient, cloud icon (s12, s15). When it reaches me the flight ends (s15b). After the run I'm told that
perfect landings push it back (s16). That should be taught during the lesson, not afterwards.

**3. Where I'm going.** Along a coast with lighthouses: Gull Point 4 km, Saltlight 9, Kestrel Head 15, Old Harry 22,
Wren Ness 30, Lantern Rock 38 (s18). Each flight starts from the pier, and each lighthouse is a new best to beat.

**4. Success and failure.** Success means distance, passing lighthouses, Perfect landings, and finishing goals that
unlock birds, skies and trails (s16, s19). Failure means the storm catches you ("Flight over", s15b). A badly timed
fold or open costs speed, though the game never actually says that (s06).

## Finish: judged harshly

The world looks good. The sunset gradients, the wave shapes and the rain sheets are calm and well put together, and
using the two halves as two wings is a lovely idea. Around that, the hint layer and the progression screens feel like
working drafts.

### Every flaw I noticed
- **s01**: Two gulls at once, a big top-down one and a small side-view one. It takes a moment to see which one I am.
  Rain falls across the trophy and settings buttons.
- **s02 vs s03**: The phone icon for "Hold it open" and "Fold a little" is identical. A diagram that says "fold" but
  shows a flat phone fails at its only job.
- **s03**: "Skip" appears only on the second step, not the first.
- **s04**: The icon turns from blue to beige with no label. I can't tell if beige means "correct", "done" or "hold".
- **s02-s04 vs s05-s08**: The label sits under the icon on some screens and above it on others. On s07 and s08 there
  is no icon at all. The hint style changes four times in six screens.
- **s04 to s05**: The scene jumps from a stormy sunset to a clear blue day with no transition. The progress dots go
  from 1 straight to 3.
- **s05/s06/s10**: A tiny yellow book icon sits on the gull's back. It's illegible at that size and clutters the bird.
- **s06** (a failure): "Stay folded" with a dashed outline. It doesn't say what I did wrong or what it cost me.
- **s07/s10**: A yellow outline "ghost" gull trails behind me, and nothing explains it.
- **s07/s08/s10/s11**: The labels float in empty sky or over the horizon, away from what they refer to.
- **s08**: A half-cropped yellow ring at the bottom-left edge collides with the Skip button.
- **s09** (the Perfect): No "Perfect!" text, no flash, nothing different from a normal landing apart from more spray.
  This is the most important moment of feedback in the game, and it doesn't land.
- **s10**: "One per wave" is cryptic. The teal phone with chevrons at the far left is a third hint style, and it's
  nowhere near the bird.
- **s11**: "Stay ahead", but there is no storm on screen and no meter yet. The meter arrives a beat later (s12).
- **s12/s13**: Gull Point's lighthouse sits on a pale, translucent rectangle that looks like a rendering bug rather
  than a rock. s12 and s13 are identical, so the lesson stalls with no clear end.
- **s14**: The gull is drawn over the countdown circle. "Storm" labels the meter in s15 but not in s14.
- **s15**: The "<" in the "NEXT Gull Point" pill reads as a Back button. It's supposed to show direction.
  The distance is in metres here but km everywhere else.
- **s15b**: Getting caught is the climax of the run, and it's a small "Flight over" pill. No lightning, no
  slow-motion, no bird reaction. The pause button and storm meter just disappear.
- **s16**: It says "You made it past Saltlight!" but I also passed Gull Point, which isn't mentioned. "New lighthouse"
  is a sticker-style badge in a different tone. The right-hand card says "New bird: Tern", but the picture is the
  same gull. The two cards aren't aligned at the top. There are three ways to fly again ("Fly again", "Fly as Tern",
  "fold and open") competing on one screen.
- **s17**: The "New" badge covers the word "Birds" ("Bird" is clipped). The Birds tile still says "Gull" after I
  unlocked Tern. Trophy and Goals look like two versions of the same idea. A hard horizontal band cuts across the sky.
- **s18**: The lighthouses ride on one wave line while my path runs on a second line underneath, so it's two
  timelines. The bottom half of the screen is empty. "Lantern Rock" is cut off at the right edge.
- **s19**: The results said Tern was unlocked, but Goals lists Tern as the reward for Level 2, which is "2 of 3 done".
  Which is true? "Next rank: Coral" mixes up the rank (Spray Chaser) with its reward (Coral). The names Puddle Hopper,
  Crest Hopper, Gull Wing and Tern Turner blur together with the bird names. Ticked goals show both a check and a full
  bar, which is redundant. The bottom row is cut off.
- **Overall**: There are three progress systems (lighthouses, levels and ranks, trophies) plus a Daily Flight after one
  run. That's too much meta for a meditative flying game.

### Compared with Monument Valley and Alto
Monument Valley teaches with no words and one consistent visual language, and every interaction gets a crafted
response. Alto teaches a single input in seconds, and its rewards, menus and art feel like one object. Swoop's world
art gets close to Alto, but its teaching uses inconsistent diagrams and floating captions. Its two key moments, the
Perfect and getting caught, are under-designed, and its menus are a mobile-game meta stack, not an Apple-style object.

### The 3 changes that would raise quality most
1. **One hint system.** Use a single animated Duo diagram that actually shows the fold angle, always next to the bird
   and in the same position, colour and type. Show it only when needed, explain mistakes ("Opened too early - lost
   speed"), and teach the storm and "perfects push it back" during the lesson.
2. **Make the Perfect and the catch unmissable.** Give the Perfect a word, a flash, haptics and the storm meter
   visibly jumping back. Give the catch real drama: rain closing in, lightning, the bird tumbling. These two beats are
   the game.
3. **Cut the meta down to one track.** The lighthouse route is the progression, and birds and skies unlock at
   lighthouses. Fix the Tern/level contradiction, the clipped badge, cropped labels and misaligned cards. Remove
   Trophy or merge it into Goals.

### Would I recommend it?
Yes, to other Duo owners, cautiously. At £1.99 the core fold-to-dive idea is a genuinely good use of the hardware, and
it's pretty. I wouldn't put it next to Monument Valley or Alto yet: it reads as 80% finished.

## Scores
- Understood: **7/10**
- Feels finished / premium: **5/10**
- I'd keep playing: **6/10**
